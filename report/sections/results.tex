\Cref{tab:evaluation} is the main result of the evaluation. \emph{The plan} takes
Reynolds' flock from no success to 8 of 10 on \texttt{dead\_end} and 10 of 10
on \texttt{l\_turn} ($p = 0.008$ and $0.002$): the corner trap is steering
straight at C, not Reynolds' rules (H2). For the other two models the plan
changes little ($p \ge 0.37$): on \texttt{l\_turn} their flocks' momentum
already carries the leaders round the corner. \emph{The models} rank
differently on each map: without a plan the hybrid and Couzin beat Reynolds
on \texttt{l\_turn} ($p \le 0.004$); with it, on \texttt{dead\_end}, Reynolds
leads with 8, Couzin 6, the hybrid 2 (hybrid against Reynolds 6 seeds to
none, $p = 0.03$). In the hybrid's failures only its leaders are in C at the
limit; \cref{sec:split} shows why.

\videofigure{video_reynolds}{Reynolds
  with the plan on \texttt{dead\_end}: 2 leaders (red) take the flock over the
  cup to C (yellow), at 40\,s of 83.}{fig:video-reynolds}

\Cref{fig:evaluation-closest} shows the \emph{spacing}. Reynolds keeps its
drones about 0.5\,m apart and never below 0.33\,m. The hybrid and Couzin
reach touching distance in many runs, and only Couzin crashed (4 runs of
40). So Couzin matches the hybrid on success but not the wider spacing H3
expected. The plan widens the hybrid's spacing on both maps (Wilcoxon,
$p \le 0.004$): a flock pressed into a wall is a compressed flock.

\Cref{tab:leaders} is the main result of the second experiment, and
\cref{fig:sweep-success} splits it by flock size. \emph{No
leaders, no arrival}: none of the 90 runs with $j = 0$ reached C, so every
success is the leaders' doing. \emph{More leaders} help as
Couzin et al.\ predict in Couzin's model only: 7, 16 and 28 of 30 for 1,
2 and 4 leaders ($p = 0.02$ and $0.002$ for each step); with one leader its
drones pack to touching (median closest pair 0.10\,m) and don't follow.
Reynolds hardly depends on $j$ (every pair $p \ge 0.07$). The hybrid does
worse with 2 leaders than with 1 (9 against 23, $p = 0.0001$) and recovers
with 4. \emph{Larger flocks} succeed less in every model, most sharply
Reynolds at $n = 30$ (4 of 30 against 25 at $n = 20$). Successful runs take
47--60\,s in median; Reynolds keeps its 0.5\,m spacing throughout.

\Cref{fig:video-couzin} shows why Couzin's flock needs more leaders. A Couzin
drone steers by its neighbours' positions and directions and by its own, so
a single leader is one voice among twenty: it turns toward C, the others
average it out, and it leaves over the cup alone, while the flock, packed
tight, wanders round A and sets off only after about 70\,s, too late. With
four leaders the goal outweighs the flock's own direction: the flock follows
the three that go over the cup into C, and the fourth, which planned round
the other arm, goes alone.

  its flock stays behind in A; 4 leaders outvote the flock's own direction

\subsection{Why two leaders lose the hybrid's flock}
\label{sec:split}

\Cref{fig:split} shows one spawn, seed 4, where the hybrid fails and
Reynolds succeeds. The two leaders start on either side of the cup's axis
and each plans round its own arm; their paths part slowly, so at go time
both head roughly east. The hybrid's followers cruise along their
neighbours' mean heading and are at full speed within 2\,s: the two
leaders' pulls cancel, and the flock flies straight on between them into
the cup. Reynolds' followers have no cruise and speed up only as they are
pulled (0.34\,m/s at 18\,s against the hybrid's 0.99), so the leaders get
apart before the flock commits, and it goes with one of them. The hybrid's
flock then turns back out of the cup and loops round the room, carrying one
leader with it, since a hybrid leader also follows its neighbours' heading;
the other leader reaches C alone. Every hybrid failure with split leaders
that was replayed (seeds 0, 4, 6, 8 and 9) goes this way.

A* is deterministic and a leader plans from where it spawned, so the side
each leader took can be recovered for every run (\cref{tab:split}). Only the
hybrid is broken by the split; with both leaders on one side it succeeds as
often as the others. With 4 leaders it recovers, even on a 2--2 split (8 of
12), which this analysis does not explain.
