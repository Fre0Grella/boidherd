\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A flock of $n$ drones takes off in sector A of a known course and has to
reach sector C. Only $j$ of them, the leaders, know where C is, and nothing
tells the others which drones those are: no flag, no signal, only neighbours
flying close by. Can the leaders bring the whole flock to C, and what does
that depend on? \citet{couzin2005} asked this of animal groups; this project
asks it of a swarm of simulated quadrotors.

The report comes with videos of the runs: click a video figure's image, or
scan its QR code, to open the videos folder, where each video is named
after its figure (\cref{fig:video-forest}).

\videofigure{video_l_hall}{20
  drones through a forest of pillars on \texttt{l\_hall}, on their way to C;
  only the 2 red ones know the way.}{fig:video-forest}

The project builds
one Crazyflie controller in Webots in which three flocking models are
interchangeable, gives the leaders an optional planning layer, and compares
models, planning, flock size and number of leaders on maps none of the
models was tuned on.

\Cref{sec:system} presents the system and its design choices, \cref{sec:controllers} the flight layer,
the models and the planner. \Cref{sec:method,sec:results,sec:discussion} give
the experiments, their results and what they mean, and \cref{sec:conclusion}
concludes.
