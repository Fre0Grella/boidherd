\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A flock of $n$ drones takes off in sector A of a known course and has to
reach sector C. Only $j$ of the drones, the leaders, know where C is, and
nothing tells the others which drones those are: there is no leader flag and
no signal, only neighbours flying close by. Can the leaders bring the whole
flock to C, and what does that depend on?

This is the question \citet{couzin2005} asked of animal groups, put to a swarm
of simulated quadrotors. It joins three things that are usually studied apart.
The first is the flocking model: the rules that tell a drone how to move from
what it senses of its neighbours, here three of them, from computer animation
\citep{reynolds1987}, from biology \citep{couzin2002,couzin2005} and from
real drone swarms \citep{vasarhelyi2018}. The second is the robot: a
Crazyflie in Webots, with the inertia, response time and limited thrust that
an ideal boid doesn't have. The third is the leaders' control: a leader that
steers straight at C leads its flock into any wall in the way, so the leaders
get a deliberative layer, a planner that finds a path on the map, while the
followers stay purely reactive.

The project compares the three models on the same robot, with the same
leaders, on maps none of them was tuned on, with the leaders' planner off and
on, and then over the number of drones and of leaders: 450 runs in all, paired
by starting position and analysed with exact tests. The hypotheses were
written down before each experiment (\cref{sec:hypotheses}).

The main findings are these. A planner is what lets a flock that follows its
leaders faithfully, Reynolds', get past a corner or a dead end at all. More
leaders help as Couzin predicted for Couzin's model only. And with two leaders
that plan their ways independently, the model built for real drones fails
where the simplest model succeeds: its flock follows the average of two paths
round an obstacle, straight into it, as Couzin's informed groups do when their
preferences differ little. Which model works best depends on what drives a
follower, and on whether the leaders agree.
